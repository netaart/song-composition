# Song form and phrases

Choose the form before you write notes. A form is a list of sections, and each section is a whole number of phrases. If the bar count is wrong, the words and the harmony will be wrong too.

## Start with a phrase

A phrase is one musical sentence. It usually ends with a cadence and a breath for the singer.

| Phrase length | In 4/4 | In 3/4 | In 6/8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 bars | 8 beats | 6 beats | 4 dotted-quarter beats |
| 4 bars | 16 beats | 12 beats | 8 dotted-quarter beats |
| 8 bars | 32 beats | 24 beats | 16 dotted-quarter beats |

Four bars is the default. Two bars is a short answer or a tag. Eight bars is a long phrase, usually heard as two four-bar phrases; it is common for a verse or a chorus in a slow song.

A section should contain a whole number of phrases. A 16-bar verse is four four-bar phrases. It is not three five-bar phrases.

When you turn the plan into a score, read `WRITING.md` for the block shape before you write the first phrase. A four-bar phrase is a natural unit.

## Common forms

| Form | Sections and bars | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short verse | 8 bars: 2 × 4 | A first verse, a folk song, a sketch |
| Long verse | 16 bars: 4 × 4 | A pop or rock verse with a development |
| Short chorus | 8 bars: 2 × 4 | A hook that repeats often |
| Long chorus | 16 bars: 4 × 4 | A chorus with a lift and a tag |
| AABA | 32 bars: 4 × 8 | A standard, a ballad, a jazz-influenced song |
| Verse–chorus | Intro, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, bridge, chorus, outro | The default pop shape |

A starting plan for a three-minute pop song:

| Section | Bars | Phrase layout | Job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intro | 4–8 | 1–2 phrases | State the groove, not the hook |
| Verse 1 | 8–16 | 2–4 × 4 | Set the scene, keep the range low |
| Pre-chorus | 4–8 | 1–2 × 4 | Build tension, end on a chord that wants the chorus |
| Chorus | 8–16 | 2–4 × 4 | Deliver the hook, raise the range |
| Verse 2 | 8–16 | 2–4 × 4 | Same shape as verse 1, new words |
| Chorus | 8–16 | 2–4 × 4 | Repeat, often with more instruments |
| Bridge | 4–8 | 1–2 × 4 | New harmony or new register |
| Final chorus | 8–16 | 2–4 × 4 | The biggest version, one extra lift |
| Outro | 4–8 | 1–2 phrases | Land the tonic, then stop |

Cut before you add. If the song feels long, remove one four-bar phrase. If it feels short, repeat the last phrase with a new ending.

## Sections and their jobs

- **Intro.** Set the tempo, the key and the texture. Four or eight bars. Do not give away the whole chorus.
- **Verse.** Tell the story. Keep the range narrow and the texture light. End the last verse phrase on a chord that leads forward.
- **Pre-chorus.** Optional. Four or eight bars. Raise the energy and shorten the chords. End on a dominant or a suspended chord.
- **Chorus.** The hook. The words and the melody should repeat. The range is higher than the verse. End on the tonic or a strong plagal cadence.
- **Bridge.** Eight bars is common. Change the harmony, the register or the groove. Prepare the return to the final chorus.
- **Outro.** Repeat a short tag, then land. Four or eight bars. Do not fade out in the score unless the style asks for a fade.

## Parallel phrases

Two phrases in a section often share a rhythm and differ at the end:

- Phrase A ends on the dominant or a non-tonic chord. It leaves the question open.
- Phrase B repeats the rhythm and ends on the tonic. It answers.

The verse example does this. Bars 1–4 end on D, the dominant of G. Bars 5–8 end on G, the tonic:

```
G | Em | C | D   (open)
C | G  | D | G   (closed)
```

The rhythm of the first phrase and the second phrase is close, so the pair sounds like one section.

## Bar counts

Count bars, not beats, when you plan a form. Then check the beats in each bar against the meter.

- A 4/4 bar has four quarter-note beats.
- A 3/4 bar has three quarter-note beats.
- A 6/8 bar has six eighth-note beats, felt as two dotted-quarter beats.

A 16-bar section in 4/4 is 64 quarter-note beats. At 120 BPM that is 32 seconds. At 60 BPM it is 64 seconds. The same bar count can be too short or too long at a different tempo, so choose the tempo with the form.

| Bars | Phrase | Harmony | Melody | Words |

| 1–4 | A, open | G–Em–C–D | Down, then up to A | "I have been walking all night long" |
| 5–8 | B, closed | C–G–D–G | Up, then down to G | "The road is carrying me home" |

## Check the form

Before you write notes, answer these:

- How many bars is each section?
- How many phrases are in each section, and how long is each phrase?
- Which phrase is the hook?
- Where is the highest note, and which section owns it?
- Does the last chorus differ from the first?
- Is there an intro and an outro, and are they short enough?

If the answer to any of these is "I will see", the form is not planned yet.
